Cognitive Dynamics Studio

Four Interactive Models of Cognition.

Explore four computational visualizations of causal, dialectical, panoramic, and synergetic dynamics. The simulations are the centerpiece: pause them, step through states, change speed, inspect live variables, and open any model fullscreen.

Enter the simulation studio ↓
01 · Live simulation studio

See the four models in motion.

Each model has a distinct visual grammar, state sequence, color system, and explanatory panel.

02 · How they work

Four models, four visual languages.

Causal–Determinist

Follow a causal chain.

Observation and premise lead through mechanism, process, deduction, and result in a directional sequence.

Dialectical–Algorithmic

Hold opposites together.

Thesis and antithesis move through contradiction toward synthesis, rule formation, and algorithmic execution.

Holographical–Panoramic

Change the viewpoint.

A stable conceptual object remains central while changing projections make different aspects visible.

Vortical–Synergetic

Watch a dynamic field.

Alternatives fluctuate, are screened, and gather around an attractor as feedback changes the field.

03 · Experiment studio

Run a reproducible computational experiment.

Seeds and steps control the software model. The resulting states describe the simulation, not a measurement of a person's cognition. 



  









  


    

      04 · Scientific integrity
    


    

      Clear boundaries between source,
      software, and evidence.
    



    



      


        

          Source-derived
        


        

          Conceptual terminology and framing are identified
          as source-derived where applicable.
        


      



      


        

          Original implementation
        


        

          Numerical variables, transition rules, trajectories,
          particles, animation, and interface are software
          representations created for this studio.
        


      



      


        

          Evidence boundary
        


        

          Simulation output is an output of the computational model.
          It is not automatically empirical evidence for a theory
          of human cognition.
        


      



      


        

          Educational purpose
        


        

          This is an educational interactive laboratory,
          not a diagnostic tool, clinical instrument, or
          validated psychological assessment.
